# On History of Intelligence

I just finished reading a book called A Brief History of Intelligence, published 3 years ago, written by Max Bennett. The book talks about the evolutionary journey of biological intelligence and its five key breakthroughs.

My learning sharing this week would be based upon that. Given the time constraints, I’ll skip some chapters. 

At the beginning, we only had reflexes. Early sea creatures just reacted, something touches you, you twitch. No direction, no goals. Then came the first breakthrough: steering. A worm-like creature could move toward things and away from things. And to do that, it needed one simple ability: tag everything as good or bad. Food is good, go there, danger is bad, get out. It can’t learn. It can’t remember. It can’t think. But for the first time, an animal has a direction.

Hundreds of million years passed. Now we’re in the age of the dinosaurs. And our ancestors are small, mouse-sized, furry, and very much on the menu. At this point you can’t just move toward good and away from bad. That’s not enough. Move at the wrong moment and you’re dinner. So these early mammals hide in caves, come out at night, and wait. And while waiting, they developed something new: they could run the world inside their head. “If I go out this way, and a predator is over there, then what happens?”

That’s the third breakthrough. And it may be the most dramatic one in the whole story.

The last breakthrough happened about 100,000 years ago. That’s language. The crown jewel. And it’s not mainly about talking to each other, for the first time, what one brain learned didn’t die with that brain. Intelligence stopped living inside a single skull and started living between people, across generations.

What I keep thinking about is how this compares to AI right now. 

Because AI seems to be running the evolution backwards. It got language first hence the name, large language models. The language problem has pretty much solved at this point. 

Next, the world model. Last week Fei-Fei Li’s company World Labs released Atlas, a system that builds 3D worlds it can move through and come back to. That’s the simulation breakthrough. 

And then? Will our first breakthrough be their last? The one where you tag things good or bad. Where you decide to make the move for yourself? And what would that decision be?

I’ll stop there. Great book, highly recommend it.
