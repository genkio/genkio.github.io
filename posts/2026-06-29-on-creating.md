# On Creating

I'd like to open by sharing a secret of mine that I've been keeping for two years. Two years ago, we had our first ever hackathon. Thoma and I did the Pafin TV, and we won 2nd place with a 10,000 yen each, which I spent on the new fridge for my apartment. To be honest, I vibe coded the whole thing using Sonnet 3.5 back then. I didn't even tell Thoma about it, cause I couldn't afford to lose that prize money, it's close to summer, my old fridge broke down all of the sudden, I needed the new one very very badly.

Two years later, as I already shared during the hackathon presentation, I did it again. This time feels a bit different though. For one, the fact that I can say it out loud, of course. But there's more to it, and that is my "what I learned" sharing today.

In the first hackathon, though I didn't write a single line of code, I had to hand-hold Sonnet 3.5 the whole time, there's zero vibe to the whole process. I was still very excited to see it could actually get things done under supervision.

As a matter of fact, the last two years have been some of my happiest and most productive days in my coding journey. Creating things from scratch using code is one of my biggest passions, the problem has always been that I didn't have enough time, with work, family everything, but things started to change two years ago.

So I did. With this supervised AI coding approach, I built 8 mini apps, each tailored to a specific aspect of my Japanese learning. There's the video player with immersion features I didn't see anywhere else, there's the kanji analyzor with built-in spaced repetition, which I still use on daily basis, there's AI-assisted novel writer, and so on.

Fast forward to two weeks ago. As I mentioned, the prototype of the Sumaten app was built on my phone. I drafted the prompt while on the train to the Yokohama Burger Festival, sent it out upon arrival, and by the time I sat down with my burger, it had long finished and was waiting for me to test. The burger was good, but there was something missing, the joy of creating things, cause it kinda felt being left out.

Looking at it from positive angle, maybe that's a good thing, maybe the best thing could happen to me in this AI age, is allowing me spend less time in front of the computer, instead of creating those 8 apps, which didn't get me very far in terms of improving my Japanese to be honest, rather, to get my butt out of the house, and start using the language, so, all good.

The end.
