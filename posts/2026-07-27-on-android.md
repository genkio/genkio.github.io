# On Android

Take a break from AI and share something else that I learned.

Android.

My very first smartphone was actually an Android phone, the Nexus S. Then the iPhone 5S came out, and it was just too light and too pretty not to switch. After that, I was a loyal iPhone user for the last ten plus years and never looked back.

Recently, because of AI development, I figured there is much more I can do with the help of agents on an Android device, something Guillaume and Alex touched on briefly.

So I bought a Pixel 8a on PayPay Flea Market for about ¥35,000, a fifth of what I paid for my iPhone. Since it's a secondary device, I figured, why not go one step further? So I installed GrapheneOS on it, replacing the stock Android OS. For the iPhone users out there, GrapheneOS is a very unusual custom ROM in the Android ecosystem. It basically removes all Google services, no ads, no tracking, the cleanest Android experience you could have. Some people even say that if you use GrapheneOS, you are either someone who cares extremely about privacy, or you are a drug dealer. I don't consider myself either of those, but I do like the idea of a very clean mobile OS.

It is so good. Right out of the box, there are only about 12 or 13 essential apps installed. There are no Google services whatsoever, and the stock apps come with black-and-white icons. Everything looks incredibly simple and clean, and I was hooked right away.

Among all the neat features, my current favorite one is that, it allows you to create multiple profiles, and each profile is completely independent of the others. Once you exit a profile, everything running inside it gets terminated.

Here is how I have set up my profiles:

The Owner profile, where I only install popular, stable open-source Android apps.

A Private Space, which works like a separate profile, where I keep the apps that I don't really want to install but still need to use. Once the Private Space is locked, all running apps inside are also terminated. There are no notifications and no background jobs whatsoever.

Plus a sandbox profile, where I experiment with new or fishy, untrusted apps.

Last time, Jérémie talked about the "frictionful lifestyle." GrapheneOS is definitely, as far as I can tell, the peak of the frictionful life in the smartphone realm.

Of course, there are some trade-offs, nothing is perfect. Things like Google Wallet just don't work. I also noticed that Google Maps doesn't really work indoors either, and it takes a while to pick up satellites when you're outside.

So, have I decided to make the final switch and put my iPhone onto PayPay Flea Market?

If you had asked me this a month ago, my answer would have been a positive yes. But a recent experience made me rethink that decision.

One Monday night a few weeks back, I got home around 9:30, and standing in front of the door, I realized I had left my keys in the office. I rushed back, but the office building was locked. So there I was, standing downstairs, thinking about what to do.

I panicked a little, because I only had my iPhone with me at that moment, but only briefly, because I had my iPhone with me.

I pulled it out, used Google Maps' precise location to find a decently priced hotel in the area, and paid for it with Apple Wallet where all my credit cards including mynumber card are safely stored, so the entire experience was incredibly smooth. I got to the hotel very quickly, and since I didn't have my laptop with me that night, I went to bed right away and slept like a baby.

As pleasantly surprised as I was by Android on GrapheneOS, and as much as I really want to pursue a digital minimalism lifestyle, but, when things go south, I think I would need a device I can absolutely rely on.

That night, it was just me alone. Let's say I had made the switch to Android on GrapheneOS. Worst case, I end up sleeping in the park, I would actually consider that an adventure. But imagine if my wife had been with me, her phone battery somehow died, and I was the only one holding a smart device, one that couldn't pinpoint our location or pay for the hotel. My mind just blocked me from thinking any further about how she would react. I would probably have to smash the phone into pieces on the ground just to demonstrate that I was as frustrated as she was, the good news is, it's quite affordable, and I probably already have an impeccable open sourced cloud backup set up already, but still, I don't think I could walk out of that situation in one piece.

So I think I'm probably going to stay with my iPhone a little bit longer, and keep an eye on it, to see when the GrapheneOS team figures out how to get apps like Google Wallet working.
