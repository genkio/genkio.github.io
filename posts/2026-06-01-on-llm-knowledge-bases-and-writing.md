# On LLM Knowledge Bases and Writing

In the very early days of my coding journey, i used to write technical blogs, in recent years switched to logseq, a local markdown note taking app, similar to obsidian, but much less known, i picked it cause i like it how it treats tags and blocks as first-class citizens, the same as pages, so that i can drop random thoughts or snippets without having too much mental burden.

Like a month ago, Andrej Karpathy shared another idea of llm knowledge base, which Emilien also talked about, all of a sudden, there's tons of videos on youtube talking about how to adapt it and build your second brain with it, fomo got to me, i like the idea of having another brain, the more the better, so i gave it a try. fast forward to last week, i gave it up, what i learn out of this experiment is that, the easier you can store the quote-unquote, knowledge, the harder, at least for me to ever go back and revisit them.

After stopped building my second brain, there’s no more this or that changes everything types of knowledge article nor code snippets in my notebook. The only thing that sticks around are my random thoughts and writings, actually, i've been doing it more often than i used to, the weekly learning sharing note that i always wrote beforehand, is one of them, these thoughts of mine, laughable as some of them might be, are what made me, for better or worse, me. i think we all agree by now, how important context is to our coding agents, and based on what i share last week, i would say, years down the road, there is a slight chance that i might end up alone with my ai companion, which hopefully, with the looks of my dog, when that day comes, these notes would be my context, that i would hand over to my ai buddy the moment it's turned on, these would be my golden ticket to the happily ever after.
